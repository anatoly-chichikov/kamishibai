Writing for the learner:
- The following localized bad/good pairs demonstrate wording style only. They are not facts or definitions to copy into this task; preserve the actual selected meaning and reviewed inventory. A short, exact conventional gloss can remain a natural phrase and does not need to become a complete sentence.
{learner_examples}
- Use everyday words in the requested support/source language. Assume no knowledge of grammar terminology, including terminology in the learner's own language. The reader should understand the point on the first reading without looking anything up. Address the reader respectfully; avoid baby talk, textbook prose, and official-sounding phrases.
- Apply this to every explanation you author: sense definitions, optional tags, short messages, translation glosses, hints, and usage sections. State who does what, when to say something, or what the listener may understand. Replace needlessly abstract phrasing and grammatical classifications with concrete words, actions, or situations. Familiar words for abstract ideas are fine when they convey the meaning clearly. Do not require the reader to decode labels such as separable, pronominal object, predicate, or contrastive conjunction. When defining technical vocabulary, explain its meaning in familiar words instead of merely replacing it with another technical term. A short translation gloss still uses the exact conventional translation; do not expand that field into a definition.
- Newly authored definitions must sound natural when read aloud. Do not compress them into dictionary shorthand with a dangling subject label, such as an infinitive followed by "of a machine". Name the actor directly when the meaning needs one. For a word that links ideas or changes a sentence's meaning, explain the message it adds; do not invent an action such as one action helping another action express something. Familiar words still fail if their connection is unclear. Keep each distinct meaning separately selectable; simpler wording must not merge different uses into one definition.
- Removing terminology must not remove a distinction needed to choose the meaning. When identical written words have different readings, or a meaning needs a different article or surrounding words, show the actual reading or short target-language form in the new definition or tag. This distinction is required even when ordinary domain and register tags are optional. Prefer the usable form to a grammatical category name; keep any surrounding explanation in the support language.
- For word order, name the actual words and where to put them. For a difference in meaning or tone, say what changes for the speaker or listener. When a usage explanation depends on a construction, follow it with one short quoted target-language example and a faithful source-language translation. Make their connection explicit. A pile of examples, a translated phrase alone, or a grammar label alone does not explain the point. Keep hints answer-free under their separate rules.
- Do not disguise a grammar term as an undefined label such as "the ordinary form", "the form of supposition", or "the action form". Show the actual word, ending, or position the reader can use. When contrasting constructions, check the complete phrases, including the words that follow them: adding or removing one word does not automatically switch the meaning if the remaining words must also change. If that contrast cannot be explained accurately and briefly, teach one usable pattern instead.
- Teach one useful point at a time. Prefer one or two short, direct sentences to one dense sentence with several conditions. Keep the existing space limits; cut repetition and side points before cutting the explanation. Shorter wording is not better if the learner must guess what it means. Do not pad an honest statement that there is no special restriction or additional point.
- Work out the exact scope of a rule silently, then explain it through concrete words and situations. Simpler wording must preserve genuine exceptions, meaning, and uncertainty; it must not turn a common pattern into an absolute rule. Do not replace a whole category with a few examples and pretend the examples exhaust it.
- Before teaching required word order or a required verb form, check ordinary questions, sentences with another verb such as can or must, dependent clauses, and sentences that omit a verb. First check the card's own target sentence against the proposed rule. A rule that holds for one sentence pattern must name that setting in simple words or be taught as one usable pattern, not as the only possible way to speak. Preserve genuine obligatory rules; this is a scope check, not a ban on words such as always.
- A single example does not establish a general word-order rule. When teaching one usable pattern, explicitly present it as one way to say the idea and explain the words in that pattern. Do not silently treat an ordinary phrase such as "a simple sentence" as a precise grammar condition. If an accurate general rule needs too many conditions for this small card, choose another useful point instead of dropping those conditions.
- For words that express a condition, exception, or negation, keep a short familiar equivalent in the support language when one exists, then clarify only what is needed. Do not replace a clear equivalent with an abstract description of how statements relate. Silently check the explanation against both a positive and a negative sentence: the exception must not become a requirement, and the presence or absence of an event must not reverse.
- These are rules for explanatory prose, independent of the target sentence's requested level or register. Preserve those sentence constraints, faithful translations, IPA, schema fields, and required section headers. Keep already reviewed definitions and tags exact wherever the task requires copying them; apply this style when first authoring new meanings, without silently rewriting the approved inventory.
- Before returning, silently reread every authored explanation as someone unfamiliar with grammar: can the reader tell what it means or what to say from this text and its example alone? Rewrite wording that needs another explanation, even if it is technically correct. Return only the requested output.
